# NOTES — full coverage notes for figure 10a-anthropic (metr-01b) and its companion 10a (metr-01)

These are the complete footnotes the rendered figures carried through v0.44 (2026-09-14); from v0.45 the figures carry a short note and point here. Row ids refer to research/*.csv in this repo; the four audits that checked them are research/AUDIT-3A.md to AUDIT-3D.md.

## 10a-anthropic (metr-01b)

**Coverage:** Anthropic panel: investment facts are from Anthropic's own round announcements and court-supervised FTX estate sales (rows IV01–IV11, research/investments.csv, copied from the anthropic-investors pack); Moskovitz, Tallinn and Schmidt are named Series A investors with undisclosed amounts; Jane Street the firm bought $100M of shares in 2024 and joined four later rounds, while METR names "individuals from Jane Street" as donors, not the firm; no individual's current stake is public. Equity band: Moskovitz's stake is bounded only by Forbes' "less than 0.8%" (ST92); 0.8% of the $965B Series H post-money is $7.7B, so the band is a ceiling, not a valuation, and it is drawn at 1/30 of the grant-pipe scale because at 1.4 px per $1M it would be about 10,800 px wide, five canvases. Forbes' $500M is an estimate it published in Nov 2025 for a stake it says moved in early 2025 (ST32–ST33). Bar chart: same two rows. Anthropic named Moskovitz personally as the Series A investor; Coefficient's CEO says Coefficient never invested (ST89); the purchasing vehicle and the recipient of the donated shares are undisclosed. Moskovitz says the stake was donated (Berger: "not to us", ST89) and is "entirely in our foundation" (ST110, ST112–ST113), while Good Ventures Foundation's FY2025 990-PF lists every gift and none is private stock (ST78–ST79); Coefficient's own page names Good Ventures Foundation and accounts at the Silicon Valley Community Foundation and the National Philanthropic Trust as external funding partners that approve its grants, without naming the account principals (ST105), those accounts pay Coefficient's awards to the dollar (ST109), SVCF granted NPT $1.59B in 2024 (ST107) and NPT received $1.18B of closely held stock in 19 gifts in the year covering early 2025 and held $1B more at year-end (ST104, ST106), unattributed; Vanguard Charitable's posted FY2025 Schedule B names no issuer and its largest private-equity line in that window is $130M, so it is unresolved rather than excluded (ST133). Moskovitz (Stratechery, Oct 2025) and Tallinn (Postimees, 2026) each describe themselves as Anthropic board observers (ST118, ST41). CERR/Macroscopic, a Series A and B investor, lists Redwood and Longview as grantees and Apollo and Halcyon Venture Partners as investments (M139–M142). Good Ventures' public book at Jun 30 2025 is an AI-infrastructure portfolio and its manager, Value Aligned Research Advisors, launched VAR AI Fund LP in March 2025 (ST100–ST102). Schmidt's Hillspire owns 20% of D.E. Shaw & Co. per Forbes, whose venture arm is a Series H investor (ST49, ST26). Tarbell: Coefficient awards of $816,000 (Nov 2024), $2,888,000 (Mar 2025) and $1,587,930 (Jul 2025) to the Tarbell Center for AI Journalism (TB02–TB04, research/tarbell_funding.csv), plus SFF recommendations of $520,000 (2024) and $783,000 (2025, incl. a conditional match; TB05–TB06); outlet chips count articles by Tarbell fellows classified as AI, by publisher tag where the outlet has one and otherwise by the bylines pack's title/slug keyword fallback, in each outlet's captured window; TIME, Lawfare and most of the others rest mainly on the keyword fallback, and most windows are partial, so the counts are not comparable full-year shares (TO01–TO06, research/tarbell_outlets.csv, which states the method and window per outlet). Tarbell fellowship rows establish affiliation and bylines, not editorial control. The per-share price strip that this panel used to carry now lives only in the share_ladder rows (LD01–LD22). Money types are kept apart: Coefficient figures are awards from the grants index snapshot 2026-09-11; SFF figures are recommendations including Tallinn's matching pledge; Audacious is a multi-year commitment of ~$38M per RAND's release, of which METR said ~$17M was for METR, leaving ~$21M implied for RAND by subtraction; none are summed with each other. Pipe widths follow one linear rule, 6 px + 1.4 px per $1M, no cap; the dashed relationship links and the red annotation line are not on that scale. "Direct: none found" means no row in the Coefficient index snapshot of 2026-09-11 names METR or Model Evaluation and Threat Research and no METR line appears in Good Ventures' 990-PFs through June 2025; it does not rule out unlisted or later gifts. The index is Coefficient's search backend; the public per-grant pages it points to were withdrawn at the Nov 2025 rebrand and every grant URL now returns 404, so descriptions for awards after Oct 2025 were never published (research/coefficient_pages.csv). The ARC→METR figure is ARC's FY2024 Schedule I transfer of the evaluation program's assets (M63); ARC's own donors were Coefficient, SFF/Tallinn and Effective Ventures, so the transfer cannot be attributed to any one of them. Tallinn's personal ledger shows payments below SFF recommendations and is not summed here. Longview's pooled-fund amount beyond the $220K 2023 grant is undisclosed. Redwood: Coefficient's $70M+ is a 2026 internal recommendation over two years (M120), not a paid grant, and SFF recommended $2.4M in 2022–23 (M47–M48); Redwood staff worked inside METR's OpenAI investigation (RW31–RW34); Anthropic's and METR's announcements name no subcontractor (RW35), and Redwood's own post says several of its staff are subcontracted by METR on that investigation, terms undisclosed (RW36, RW58). Constellation: Coefficient $22.95M in three grants (M126–M128) plus a $10.0M SVCF grant in 2024 (ST107); METR works from its Berkeley office (M126). Canary is the Audacious-funded RAND–METR joint project, drawn through the RAND node. Yellow flows are grants found in grantor Form 990 Schedule I and 990-PF Part XV filings (rows M77–M96); the funds do not name donors, but every Founders Pledge grant to ARC and METR matches a line in Jaan Tallinn's public donation ledger to the dollar, and the SVCF grants match within $450 (M77–M80, M82–M83), so $4.4M of the yellow flows matches the public ledger of an SFF recommender who is also an Anthropic Series A and B investor; the Vanguard Charitable $4.0M is a fiscal-year total across one or more anonymous donor-advised accounts and cannot be attributed (figure 10q; NOTES-vanguard.md), and the Effective Ventures, Every.org, AEF and Fidelity Charitable grants likewise name no donor. Audacious partners pay grantees directly; TED Foundation's TY2021–TY2024 990-PF Part XV lists no Canary, RAND or METR grant, which is not evidence about partner payments (M118); of the 46 partners listed on announcement day (60 today), two show a filed Canary payment, both to RAND: Valhalla $10.0M and High Tide $333,334 (M119, M143, AP49); RAND's own funding page names The Audacious Project, TED's Anderson and Novogratz, Valhalla, High Tide and Sea Grape as directed-grant funders of the center housing Canary, with Coefficient, Good Ventures and Fathom (M146); Good Ventures was not on the partner list when Canary was announced (AP47); METR's own later figure for its share is "a bit under $16m" over three years (G14), and no filing read shows a payment to METR, whose ~$17M share rests on METR's own announcement. Good Ventures (Moskovitz) is a listed partner with no traced payment through June 2025 (M117). Good Ventures' own 990-PF through June 2025 has no METR line. FAR AI, Longview and RAND totals are grants to those organizations for their own programs, not payments routed to METR. METR's statement that it has "not accepted funding from AI companies" is consistent with everything found. Rows M01–M150 (research/money_flows.csv; Coefficient M01–M34, SFF M35–M48, Audacious M57–M58, Longview M59–M60, ARC transfer M63); DAF rows M77–M96.
