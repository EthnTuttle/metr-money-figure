October 2025 – September 2026 · three things in one window

METR raised $71M in the six months the evaluator's seat was being designed; the investigations came after the money, not before it

Read it in three steps: the money, the seat, the incidents. Six of nine proposals for a mandated evaluator fell inside the fundraising window; the two incident investigations were agreed at its end and after it. The overlap is at six-month resolution because METR does not date its commitments.

1 · The money
METR says it raised about $71M of commitments between mid-February and mid-August 2026 (G03). That is seven times its 2024 budget (G01). Who committed what, and when inside the window, is not public.
2 · The seat
In the same months, the industry and Washington drafted the job METR now holds: the Frontier Risk Report, OpenAI's and Anthropic's frameworks calling for independent evaluators, Hassabis's FINRA-style body, the White House review and the FRONTIER Act (markers 1–7). The three proposals that name METR came after the money was announced (8–10).
3 · The incidents
The OpenAI investigation was agreed July 30, two weeks before the announcement; the Anthropic investigation on September 9, after it. So the money cannot have followed the incidents. It arrived while the seat was being designed. Whether donors were funding the seat is an inference; the dates are not.

The two incident investigations were agreed on July 30 and September 9, at the end of and after the window; the routine risk-report reviews in March were already happening before any of it. What the commitments were for is not public; the sequence rules out the incidents as the cause and is consistent with, but does not prove, the money anticipating the seat.
