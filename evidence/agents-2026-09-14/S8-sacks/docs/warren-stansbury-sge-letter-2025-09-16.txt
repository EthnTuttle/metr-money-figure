Dear Mr. Sacks:

We are investigating whether you may have exceeded the time limit for serving in your
temporary role as the White House’s Special Advisor for AI and Crypto. We are writing to
request information regarding the number of days you have served in this role since your
appointment.

Through Craft Ventures and your other holdings, you are deeply invested in the crypto and AI
companies that you have the power to impact as the nation’s “Crypto and AI Czar.” The White
House waived ethics restrictions to allow you to maintain those investments in the industries you
regulate.1 Any effort to stay beyond the time limits imposed on you as a Special Government
Employee (SGE) would raise additional ethics concerns for you and the Trump Administration,
particularly as it moves to implement recently enacted cryptocurrency legislation and put in
place new rules for the crypto industry.

President Donald Trump appointed you to serve as an SGE. By definition, SGEs are meant to
perform “temporary duties” “for not to exceed one hundred and thirty days during any period of
three hundred and sixty-five consecutive days.”2 The Office of Government Ethics has explained
that any day that an SGE is either compensated or performs more than de minimis tasks counts
toward the 130-day limit.3 SGEs enjoy more flexible ethics requirements precisely because they
are meant to work no longer than 130 days per year. Unlike regular employees, SGEs may be
paid by private entities for time spent working for the federal government, among other

2025.6 If you have worked every calendar day since the presidential inauguration, your 130th day
of work in this role was May 29, 2025, and if you have worked every business day, your 130th
day was July 25, 2025. As of the date of this letter, it is the 167th business day of this
Administration. According to Craft Ventures, you have been “splitting [your] weeks between
D.C. and Silicon Valley to stay under the 130-day term limit as a special government employee,”
and the White House claims you have only “counted” the days you “engage[] in work as the AI
and crypto czar” towards your 130-day SGE limit.7 However, information about the exact
number of days you have served in this position is not publicly available. Concerningly, a
colleague of yours at the White House reportedly said that you have “no intention of leaving.”8

If you have indeed passed the 130th day mark, you are undermining the careful balance Congress

To better understand your compliance with the expectation that you will work for a maximum of
130 days this year, we ask that you respond to the following questions by October 1, 2025:

     1. Please provide a copy of your SF-50 Notification of Personnel Action form.
     2. How many days have you worked for the Trump Administration in 2025, either in your
        capacity as the White House’s Special Advisor for AI and Crypto or in any other
        capacity, as of the date of your response? Please provide a list of each day you have
        served in this capacity.
     3. Who in the White House has been responsible for monitoring the number of days you are
        working, in accordance with guidance from the Office of Government Ethics?13
     4. Have you been “splitting [your] weeks between D.C. and Silicon Valley” as reported,14
        and if so, how many days have you spent physically present in Washington D.C. this
        year?
            a. Of your days in Washington D.C. this year, during how many days did you
                conduct any official business?
     5. Have you conducted any duties associated with your White House role, including taking
        meetings and responding to emails, while in Silicon Valley or other locations outside of
        Washington D.C.?
     6. When in Washington D.C., have you conducted any duties associated with your White
        House role while not on the White House campus? Please list any meetings and/or
        obligations conducted outside of the White House.

Thank you for your attention to this matter.
