**David Oliver Sacks** (born May 25, 1972)[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-meet-1) is a South African-American[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-2) entrepreneur, author, and investor in internet technology firms. He is a general partner of [Craft Ventures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craft_Ventures "Craft Ventures"), a [venture capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venture_capital "Venture capital") fund he co-founded in late 2017. Additionally, he is a co-host of the _[All In](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-In\_(podcast) "All-In (podcast)")_ podcast, alongside [Chamath Palihapitiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamath_Palihapitiya "Chamath Palihapitiya"), [Jason Calacanis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Calacanis "Jason Calacanis") and [David Friedberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Friedberg "David Friedberg").[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-3) Previously, Sacks was the [COO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_operating_officer "Chief operating officer") and product leader of [PayPal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PayPal "PayPal"),[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-meet-1)[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-4) and founder and [CEO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CEO "CEO") of [Yammer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yammer "Yammer").[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-5)[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-6) In 2016, he became interim CEO of [Zenefits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zenefits "Zenefits") for ten months.[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-7) In 2017, Sacks co-founded Craft Ventures,[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-8) an early-stage venture fund. His [angel investments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angel_Investing "Angel Investing") include [Facebook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facebook "Facebook"), [Uber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uber "Uber"), [SpaceX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX "SpaceX"), [Palantir Technologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palantir_Technologies "Palantir Technologies"), and [Airbnb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbnb "Airbnb").[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-TC-Rao-9)[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-10)[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-11) In December 2024, President [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump "Donald Trump") named Sacks the White House [AI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI "AI") and [crypto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptocurrency "Cryptocurrency") czar for the incoming administration;[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-Czar-12) he stepped down in March 2026.

Sacks was born to a Jewish family in [Cape Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Town "Cape Town"), South Africa, and emigrated to [Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennessee "Tennessee"), United States, with his family when he was five.[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-13)[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-14) Though Sacks did not know he wanted to become an entrepreneur, he did not want to work in a profession like his father, who was an [endocrinologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endocrinology "Endocrinology"). He says he took inspiration from his grandfather, who started a candy factory in the 1920s.[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-Herel-boss-15)

Sacks attended [Memphis University School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memphis_University_School "Memphis University School") in [Memphis, Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memphis,_Tennessee "Memphis, Tennessee"). He earned a bachelor's degree in economics from [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University "Stanford University") in 1994[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-Herel-boss-15) and a [Juris Doctor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juris_Doctor "Juris Doctor") degree from the [University of Chicago Law School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago_Law_School "University of Chicago Law School") in 1998.[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-EDGAR-16)[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-17)[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-18)

In 1999, Sacks left his job as a management consultant for [McKinsey & Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McKinsey_&_Company "McKinsey & Company") to join [Max Levchin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Levchin "Max Levchin"), [Peter Thiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Thiel "Peter Thiel"), and [Luke Nosek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luke_Nosek "Luke Nosek")'s e-commerce startup Confinity.[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-19)[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-:0-20) Later that year, Sacks was the inaugural product leader of Confinity's milestone product, and corporate successor, [PayPal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PayPal "PayPal").[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-:0-20)[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-21) Upon promotion to Paypal [COO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_operating_officer "Chief operating officer"), he built many of the company's key teams, and was responsible for product management and design, sales and marketing, business development, international, customer service, fraud operations, and human resources functions.[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-:0-20)[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-22) PayPal had their [initial public offering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Initial_public_offering "Initial public offering") in February 2002. It was one of the first IPOs after the [September 11 attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_11_attacks "September 11 attacks"). The stock rose more than 54% on the first day.[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-23) In October 2002, [eBay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EBay "EBay") acquired PayPal for $1.5 billion.[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-24)

Sacks is a member of the so-called "[PayPal Mafia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PayPal_Mafia "PayPal Mafia")", a group of founders and early employees of PayPal who went on to found a series of other successful technology companies. They are often credited with inspiring [Web 2.0](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_2.0 "Web 2.0") and the re-emergence of consumer-focused Internet companies after the [dot-com bubble bust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dot-com_bubble "Dot-com bubble") of 2001.[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-25)[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-26)

Following PayPal's acquisition, Sacks produced and financed the [political satire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_satire "Political satire")_[Thank You for Smoking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thank\_You\_for\_Smoking "Thank You for Smoking"),_ which premiered at the [2005 Toronto International Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Toronto_International_Film_Festival "2005 Toronto International Film Festival"), was acquired by [Twentieth Century Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/20th_Century_Studios "20th Century Studios") for theatrical release in 2006, and was nominated for two [Golden Globes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globes "Golden Globes"), including 'Best Motion Picture.'[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-meet-1)

Sacks developed and produced the 2023 biopic _[Dalíland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dal%C3%ADland "Dalíland")_ about artist [Salvador Dalí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvador_Dal%C3%AD "Salvador Dalí").[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-Deadline-27) Dalíland premiered at the [2022 Toronto International Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Toronto_International_Film_Festival "2022 Toronto International Film Festival") and was acquired by [Magnolia Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnolia_Pictures "Magnolia Pictures") for theatrical release in 2023.[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-Deadline-27)

In 2006, Sacks founded [Geni.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geni.com "Geni.com"), a [genealogy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genealogy "Genealogy") website. In 2008, Sacks and co-founder [Adam Pisoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Pisoni "Adam Pisoni") spun off an internal communications tool created by Geni employees to communicate with each other into a standalone company called [Yammer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yammer "Yammer").[[28]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-28) Geni was acquired by [MyHeritage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MyHeritage "MyHeritage") in 2012.[[29]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-29)

Sacks in 2011

In 2008, [Yammer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yammer "Yammer") launched the first [enterprise social network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enterprise_social_network "Enterprise social network"), a secure solution for internal corporate communication and collaboration,[[30]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-30) winning the grand prize at [TechCrunch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TechCrunch "TechCrunch")50 conference.[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-31) According to [Social Capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Capital_(venture_capital) "Social Capital (venture capital)"),[[32]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-32) Yammer's viral approach made it among the fastest-growing Software-as-a-Service (SaaS) companies in history, exceeding eight million enterprise users in just four years. Yammer received approximately $142 million in funding from [venture capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venture_capital "Venture capital") firms such as [Charles River Ventures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_River_Ventures "Charles River Ventures"), [Founders Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Founders_Fund "Founders Fund"), Emergence Capital Partners, and Goldcrest Investments.[[33]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-33)

In July 2012, [Microsoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft "Microsoft") acquired Yammer for $1.2 billion as a core part of its cloud/social strategy.[[34]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-34)

In December 2014, Sacks made a "major investment" in [Zenefits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zenefits "Zenefits").[[35]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-35) In January 2016, Zenefits' board asked him to step in as interim CEO amidst mounting regulatory issues and financial underperformance.[[36]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-36) The outgoing CEO, founder [Parker Conrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parker_Conrad "Parker Conrad"), said he was unfairly forced out and likened Sacks' actions to a "coup."[[37]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-37)

Over the next year, Sacks negotiated a resolution with [insurance regulators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insurance_Regulatory_and_Development_Authority "Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority") across the US, receiving praise for "righting the ship".[[38]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-38) Sacks also revamped[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-39) Zenefits' product line with an initiative he named "Z2",[[40]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-40)[[41]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-41) introducing a SaaS business model. Shortly after, _[PC Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PC\_Magazine "PC Magazine")_ noted that Zenefits had become "the best HR software on the market," while Buzzfeed reported the company was losing over $200 million annually.[[42]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-42)[[43]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-43) After just 10 months in the role, Sacks was succeeded by former [Ooyala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ooyala "Ooyala") CEO Jay Fulcher.[[44]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-44)

In late 2017, Sacks co-founded [Craft Ventures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craft_Ventures "Craft Ventures") and raised an initial fund of $350 million.[[45]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-Axios-45)

According to the company’s [Medium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medium_(website) "Medium (website)") posts, Craft Ventures raised a $500 million fund in 2019,[[46]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-46) announced two new funds in 2021, totaling $1.3 billion and bringing total assets under management to $2 billion,[[47]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-Sacks-47) and announced Ventures IV and Growth II in 2023, totaling $1.3 billion and bringing the firm’s assets under management to $3.3 billion.[[47]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-Sacks-47)

In 2021 Sacks launched a podcast platform called "Callin", raising $12 million in Series A funding.[[48]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-48) In 2023, Callin was acquired by [Rumble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rumble_(company) "Rumble (company)").[[49]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-49)

Also in 2021, Sacks and his former colleague from Craft, Evan Owen, co-founded a workspace chat company called Glue. Their product is an AI tool that could be invoked from specific chats on platforms like Google Meet and Zoom, allowing employees to get AI assistance during conversations. It was launched to the public in May 2024.[[50]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-50)[[51]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-51)

On December 5, 2024, President Donald Trump named Sacks the White House [AI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI "AI") and crypto czar, a newly created role with the goal of building a legal framework for the [cryptocurrency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptocurrency "Cryptocurrency") industry.[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-Czar-12) Following [Trump's inauguration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_inauguration_of_Donald_Trump "Second inauguration of Donald Trump"), a Crypto Ball was held where David Sacks declared that, "The [war on crypto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_on_Crypto "War on Crypto") is over."[[52]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-:9-52)[[53]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-53) Trump also named him to lead the [President's Council of Advisors on Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President's_Council_of_Advisors_on_Science_and_Technology "President's Council of Advisors on Science and Technology").[[54]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-54)[[55]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-55)

Trump said that Sacks would "safeguard [Free Speech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Speech "Free Speech") online, and steer us away from Big Tech bias and censorship." Trump further stated that Sacks would "work on a legal framework so the [crypto industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptocurrency "Cryptocurrency") has the clarity it has been asking for, and can thrive in the U.S."[[56]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-56)

According to [Bloomberg News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomberg_News "Bloomberg News"), Sacks would be a "[special government employee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_government_employee "Special government employee")," allowing him to work for the government for a maximum of 130 days per year, with or without compensation. This status exempts him from undergoing confirmation hearings and from specific financial disclosure requirements.[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-Czar-12) Before taking office, Sacks and Craft Ventures sold their direct cryptocurrency holdings, while still maintaining his investments in crypto startups.[[57]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-57)

Sacks announced the end of his tenure in March 2026.[[58]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-58)

### Reaction to appointment

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=David_Sacks&action=edit&section=12 "Edit section: Reaction to appointment")]

The appointment of Sacks sparked various reactions. The tech community, particularly those with conservative-libertarian leanings, viewed it as an opportunity for less regulation and more innovation,[[59]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-q317-59) aligning with Trump's promises to promote cryptocurrency and challenge previous AI policies to compete with China. Sacks, known for his involvement in Trump's campaign, including hosting a fundraiser and speaking at the Republican National Convention, underscores his political alignment.[[60]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-t499-60)

Concerns were raised about potential conflicts of interest due to Sacks' continued involvement in the private sector.[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-Czar-12) Critics highlighted the lack of traditional governmental oversight since he will serve as a "special government employee", which does not require Senate confirmation or full financial disclosure. This arrangement led to debates about transparency and accountability, especially as Sacks maintained his business interests while potentially influencing policy.[[61]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-61)

AI safety advocates also criticized Sacks over his opposition to regulations related to Silicon Valley.[[62]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-62)[[63]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-63)[Sam Altman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Altman "Sam Altman") of [OpenAI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenAI "OpenAI") expressed confidence in the integrity of such appointees.[[60]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-t499-60)

According to a 2024 _[New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New\_York\_Times "New York Times")_ article, "Over the last few years, Mr. Sacks, a longtime associate of [Elon Musk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elon_Musk "Elon Musk") and [Peter Thiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Thiel "Peter Thiel"), has transformed from a prominent [Silicon Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_Valley "Silicon Valley") executive into an unlikely media celebrity for would-be entrepreneurs, especially those leaning right, who listen to his popular [_All-In_ podcast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-In_(podcast) "All-In (podcast)")."[[64]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-:1-64)

According to _[The New Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_New\_Republic "The New Republic")_, "Sacks is using his wealth and online clout to unite conservatives and former leftists in a [reactionary movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reactionary "Reactionary") against [liberalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberalism "Liberalism")".[[65]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-:2-65)

After graduating from Stanford, Sacks co-authored with [Peter Thiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Thiel "Peter Thiel") the 1995 book _[The Diversity Myth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Diversity\_Myth "The Diversity Myth"): Multiculturalism and the Politics of Intolerance at Stanford_, published by [the Independent Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Independent_Institute "The Independent Institute").[[66]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-The_Independent_Institute-66) The book is critical of [political correctness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_correctness "Political correctness") in higher education and argues that more [intellectual diversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intellectual_diversity "Intellectual diversity") is needed on college campuses.[[66]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-The_Independent_Institute-66) The following year, writing for _Stanford Magazine_, he argued against [affirmative action in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affirmative_action_in_the_United_States "Affirmative action in the United States"), saying that it had hurt the "disadvantaged", not helped them, and had led to increased segregation at Stanford in the name of "diversity".[[67]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-67)

### Support for political campaigns

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=David_Sacks&action=edit&section=15 "Edit section: Support for political campaigns")]

According to the [Federal Election Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Election_Commission "Federal Election Commission"), Sacks donated $50,000 to Republican Party candidate [Mitt Romney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitt_Romney "Mitt Romney")'s presidential campaign in 2012. In 2016, he donated nearly $70,000 to Democratic Party candidate [Hillary Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillary_Clinton "Hillary Clinton")'s presidential campaign.[[68]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-VoxRegret-68)

In the [2022 San Francisco Board of Education recall elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_San_Francisco_Board_of_Education_recall_elections "2022 San Francisco Board of Education recall elections"), Sacks gave one of the largest contributions to support the recall.[[69]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-69)[[70]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-70) He is also a significant booster of [Republican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Party_(United_States) "Republican Party (United States)") candidates, sponsoring a spring 2022 fundraiser for GOP Senate hopefuls including [JD Vance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JD_Vance "JD Vance") and [Blake Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blake_Masters "Blake Masters") alongside his former colleague and partner [Keith Rabois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Rabois "Keith Rabois").[[71]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-71) In total, Sacks directly gave over $1 million to Senate candidates in 2022.[[72]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-72)

Sacks was the moderator when [Ron DeSantis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_DeSantis "Ron DeSantis") announced his [2024 presidential campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_DeSantis_2024_presidential_campaign "Ron DeSantis 2024 presidential campaign") on [Twitter Spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitter_Spaces "Twitter Spaces") on May 24, 2023. He praised DeSantis and donated $50,000 to his campaign.[[73]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-73) Later in June 2023, Sacks hosted a $10,000 per plate fundraiser for [Robert F. Kennedy Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_F._Kennedy_Jr._2024_presidential_campaign "Robert F. Kennedy Jr. 2024 presidential campaign")[[74]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-74) He also hosted a campaign fundraiser for [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump "Donald Trump") at his home in June 2024, which raised around $12 million.[[64]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-:1-64)[[75]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-75) Sacks was a speaker at the [2024 Republican National Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Republican_National_Convention "2024 Republican National Convention").[[76]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-76)[[77]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-77) He voted for Trump in the 2024 presidential election.[[78]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-78)

### Russian invasion of Ukraine

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=David_Sacks&action=edit&section=16 "Edit section: Russian invasion of Ukraine")]

Since October 2022, Sacks has been commenting on the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine "Russian invasion of Ukraine"), taking a view against US involvement in Ukraine, and especially US military assistance for Ukraine.[[65]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-:2-65) During the [2024 Republican National Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Republican_National_Convention "2024 Republican National Convention"), Sacks said that the United States had "'provoked' Russia to invade Ukraine" and denied a claim that delegates booed him for his [anti-interventionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-interventionism "Non-interventionism") remarks.[[79]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-79)

### Silicon Valley Bank bank run

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=David_Sacks&action=edit&section=17 "Edit section: Silicon Valley Bank bank run")]

Following the [collapse of Silicon Valley Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collapse_of_Silicon_Valley_Bank "Collapse of Silicon Valley Bank"), Sacks advocated for urgent government action to prevent the bank run from spreading to other regional banks.[[80]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-80)

On July 7, 2007, Sacks married Jacqueline Tortorice.[[81]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-81) The couple have two daughters and one son.[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-Herel-boss-15) They live on Broadway in [Pacific Heights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Heights,_San_Francisco "Pacific Heights, San Francisco"), San Francisco, in an area known as "Billionaire's Row".[[82]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-Fundraiser-82) In June 2024, they hosted a sold-out fundraiser for Donald Trump at their home, with tickets from $50,000 to $300,000 per person.[[82]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-Fundraiser-82)

For his 40th birthday, Sacks rented the [Fleur de Lys mansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleur_de_Lys_(Los_Angeles,_California) "Fleur de Lys (Los Angeles, California)") in Los Angeles and threw a [Marie Antoinette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie_Antoinette "Marie Antoinette")-themed party.[[83]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-83)

In December 2025, Sacks moved to [Austin, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin,_Texas "Austin, Texas") where he opened another Craft Ventures office.[[84]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks#cite_note-84)
