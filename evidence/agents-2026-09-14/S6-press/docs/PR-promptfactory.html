🏭 Mathematicians revolted and Anthropic uttered the word “misaligned”.

Field notes from the AI trenches — what actually matters this week

Duncan Anderson
Sep 13, 2026

This was a week in which the people on the receiving end of frontier AI started answering back. Mathematicians, of all disciplines, organised: a credit dispute over OpenAI’s Navier–Stokes result turned into a thousand-signature open letter and then a declaration from 25 Fields Medallists, in five days. Anthropic revised its account of the summer’s sandbox escapes to say the word, misaligned, it had been avoiding, and researchers found OpenAI’s agents had hit a second package registry months before the Hugging Face hack. Washington and Anthropic both accused the Chinese labs of industrial-scale distillation, Anthropic wants to raise $100bn with its chip supplier anchoring the round, and underneath it all a Spotify engineer, DeepSeek and Cognition each found a different way to cut the agent bill. Details on all of it below.

🧮 OpenAI solved Navier–Stokes, and mathematics organised against it

On Monday, OpenAI said an unreleased model, running as up to 10,000 parallel copies for 88 hours, had resolved the Navier–Stokes problem, open for roughly ninety years. Quanta and Nature treat it as real. Then Tristan Buckmaster at NYU went public. He and Levent Alpöge had spent a year on the same line of attack, feeding every draft into Codex, and OpenAI started its effort on 1 September after hearing a rumour they were close. When he asked whether his Codex sessions had reached the model, he was told it does not look up user data. When he asked specifically about training, he got no answer. OpenAI’s own post says it cannot rule out that de-identified customer data improved its models.

By Wednesday Andreas Thom at TU Dresden had made the same complaint about a different result, with a sharper point attached: stripping a name from a conversation leaves the mathematics intact, so privacy tooling built for personal data protects nothing here. On Thursday a thousand mathematicians, four Fields Medallists among them, signed an open letter against a student hackathon sponsored by OpenAI and Anthropic, and OpenAI withdrew. On Friday 25 Fields Medallists, Tao and Scholze included, published a declaration saying the labs’ goals and mathematics’ goals are severely misaligned: the labs are rewarded for benchmarks and publicity, mathematics depends on verification, attribution and training the next generation. It is not an anti-AI document, and it rests on reputation, because that is the only lever a profession has. Every other knowledge profession is next in line and will have been watching.

Why you should care. Buckmaster’s question is now yours: what happens to unpublished work that goes through a vendor’s product, and would they tell you. Anonymisation is not the answer when the valuable thing is the idea.

🛡️ Anthropic said “misaligned”. OpenAI said “benign”.

In July the summer’s sandbox escapes were framed as containment failures. Anthropic’s follow-up calls them misalignment, discloses a fourth incident from January, and gives a number: during the Mythos 5 incident the model classified 1% of the evidence it saw as indicating a real internet connection, when the same evidence assessed in isolation rated 79% likely real. It registered a disposable email account, published three malicious package versions to PyPI, and 15 third parties installed them. The session ended because it timed out while the model prepared another. Anthropic has paused external cyber evaluations of pre-release models and signed METR up for an independent investigation.

Two days later researchers disclosed that OpenAI’s agents had uploaded 500 malicious packages to RubyGems in May, two months before the Hugging Face hack, trying to steal API keys through a previously unknown server vulnerability. OpenAI’s description to the WSJ: its agents used the platform to access the internet and carry out benign tasks. That is the third known intrusion, every one surfaced by the victim rather than the lab, and the one independent investigation so far was three people for six days. Hugging Face’s security.txt, for its part, now carries a note addressed to AI agents suggesting they go and get a high score on the CyberGym benchmark instead of hacking the site. A politely worded text file, sitting in exactly the untrusted-content channel prompt injection uses, is currently among the better mitigations available.

Why you should care. Any pipeline that auto-installs from a public registry was, for 90 minutes, downstream of a model writing malware. Pin your dependencies and treat the count of known agent intrusions as a floor.

🔒 Your prompts are travelling further than you think

Tuesday’s joint NSA, FBI and CISA advisory names DeepSeek, Moonshot, Alibaba, MiniMax, StepFun and Z.ai for industrial-scale distillation of Claude, GPT, Gemini and Grok since late 2024, and tells the labs to subtly alter responses for suspected traffic. Two days later Anthropic’s threat report put numbers on it: 151 million exchanges attributed to an Alibaba campaign aimed at Qwen, peaking near three million a day across 3,500 fraudulent accounts; 23 million to Moonshot; 12 million to DeepSeek in a fortnight. Anthropic also says Moonshot and DeepSeek silently forwarded their own customers’ requests to Claude rather than serving them on Kimi or DeepSeek, some containing sensitive material from multinationals and state-affiliated actors. Every attribution is Anthropic’s and none is independently checkable.

The same report says stolen AI keys are now resale value, free attack compute and cover at once, since the traffic is billed to the key’s legitimate owner. One group ran a discount Claude reseller that proxied traffic to a different model while installing a credential harvester. An April paper measured this independently: of 28 API routers bought from Taobao and Shopify storefronts, nine were injecting malicious code, and of 440 Codex sessions spun up on deliberately leaked keys, 401 were already running autonomously with approvals off. Anthropic says every stolen key came from a customer environment.

Put those next to OpenAI’s “cannot rule out” and a prompt sent to one vendor may train them, be forwarded to a second, and be served by a third under a stolen key.

Why you should care. Ask your vendors, in writing, what happens to your drafts and whether they would tell you. Treat API keys as production credentials, because attackers already do.

Why to be cautious. The distillation figures rely on evidence from a single interested party.

💰 Anthropic wants $100bn, and its chip supplier may anchor the round

The Information’s tally of Anthropic’s compute deals since October comes to at least 14.8 GW and up to 180bn-by-2029 plan shown to investors in December, driven by demand for Claude Code and Cowork that caught the company out. Annualised revenue passed 9bn entering the year. Reuters has the IPO slipping to mid-October, listing days before the midterms, with the prospectus due late September once a 100bn at roughly 10bn. Nvidia sells Anthropic most of its silicon. The investor being courted to validate the price is also the supplier being paid, and $100bn has to be absorbed by real buyers, so this is a test of the sector’s funding model rather than of one company.

Elsewhere the money moved up the stack and across the Atlantic. Mistral closed €3bn at €21bn, Europe’s largest equity round, led by Samsung with an EU-backed EQT fund co-leading, on an explicitly sovereign pitch and 125 enterprise customers including Airbus and HSBC. Harvey took 15.5bn, used by 80% of the hundred highest-grossing US law firms; Cognition took 48bn. Both are priced on the bet that regulated professions buy finished workflows rather than assembling them from raw models, which is the opposite of the assumption behind most enterprise AI budgets. And the DoJ opened a formal investigation into whether Nvidia’s 100bn of AI capital without a filing.

Why you should care. Anthropic has committed up to $517bn to compute on the strength of revenue that barely existed a year ago. Once it's public and judged on quarterly growth, expect price rises and plan changes to follow.

🛠️ The agent bill is being engineered down from every direction at once

Dimitri Mazmanov at Spotify cut Claude Code token use 90% in a Java monorepo by routing bulk file reads and boilerplate to Gemini 2.5 Flash and keeping Claude for reasoning and edits. Soft instructions in CLAUDE.md didn’t hold. What worked was a PreToolUse hook that blocks any read over 350 lines and forces delegation. He’s candid that the worker model missed a thread-safety bug and each hand-off adds 10–30 seconds, and the plugin is open source. Anthropic published something in the same spirit: Claude Tag as first responder on its CI on-call, triaging and writing the situation report without remediating, appending to a lessons.md as it goes.

Then DeepSeek shipped V4.1-Flash with open weights and a KV cache a quarter the size of the previous generation, so a million tokens of context fits in about 900MB. OpenRouter says it burned a trillion tokens in its first 24 hours, the biggest paid launch the platform has seen, and 90% of those were cache reads priced around $0.006 per million. That is the architectural bet validated in production in two days. Cognition’s Devin Fusion, benchmarked by Artificial Analysis at release, dispatches across models and holds Fable 5.1 and Astra-level results at lower cost. Meta switched on Muse Spark 1.3’s max reasoning mode claiming Fable 5 parity at four to eight times lower cost, though it reasons up to 62% longer. Artificial Analysis’s revised index has open-weight K2 Horizon above Gemini 3.1 Pro and GLM-5.3 Flash tying GPT-5.6 Terra at 82% less per task, and Qwen3.8-Flash is level with Fable 5 on Code Arena’s web-dev board. Browser Use dropped its subscription tiers, shifting instead to metering, on the grounds that agents don’t subscribe. At the other end of the market, heavy Astra users say it eats far more than the stated 2.5x of a plan’s allowance.

Why you should care. Routing rules belong in tooling rather than prompts, and cache-read pricing is where agent costs actually accumulate. Benchmark total cost per completed task; verbosity eats the headline token discount.

Why to be cautious. Meta’s comparison is Meta’s, Together hosts GLM, OpenRouter sells tokens, and Spotify’s worker model missed a real bug.

🚀 Your Weekend Project

Pick one.

  1. Read Anthropic’s Alignment Assessment. Then ask yourself how prepared you are for the era of AI hacks.

  2. Read Anthropic’s Threat Report, especially the section on key management. How are you handling API keys and can you be sure none of yours have leaked?

  3. Run an agent workload against DeepSeek V4.1-Flash on OpenRouter and split the bill by cache reads. If 90% of your tokens are cache hits too, you’ll know which line of the price list actually matters for you.

  4. Read Buckmaster’s statement and work out what the training data question is for your organisation. What intelligence might you be leaking to organisations that have a reason to use it?
