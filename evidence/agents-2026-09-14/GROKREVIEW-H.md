### C. The law lane (G60) — what bears on this case

**NF13. A <0.8% stake is inside the excess-business-holdings de minimis at a foundation and at a DAF alike.** G60:C (4943 rows), verified at govinfo: "(C) 2 percent de minimis rule: A private foundation shall not be treated as having excess business holdings in any corporation in which it … owns not more than 2 percent of the voting stock and not more than 2 percent in value of all outstanding shares of all classes of stock." G60 missed IRC 4943(e), verified same page: "For purposes of this section, a donor advised fund (as defined in section 4966(d)(2)) shall be treated as a private foundation." What it changes: neither Candidate 0 (Good Ventures Foundation, ST110) nor the DAF reading faces a five-year forced-sale clock; no tax rule forces the holder to disclose or divest; "less than 0.8%" (ST92) is comfortably under 2% on both tests. A gift (not a sale) of the founder's shares to the foundation is not self-dealing under 4941(d)(1)(A) (G60:C; a founder-to-foundation sale would be, even at a bargain, Treas. Reg. 53.4941(d)-2(a)(1)).

**NF14. Donor-side paper is entirely non-public.** G60:B, verified About Form 8282: "Donee organizations use this form to report information to the IRS and donors about dispositions of certain charitable deduction property made within three years after the donor contributed the property." Form 8283 (qualified appraisal required for nonpublicly traded stock over $10,000; appraisal attached over $500,000, IRC 170(f)(11)(D)) rides on the donor's 1040 (IRC 6103); Form 8282 goes to the IRS and the donor, not to 6104 inspection. What it changes: the only public documents are the recipient's 990 Schedule M/D (aggregate), its Schedule B Part II via 6104(d) (NF9), a 990-PF Schedule B (fully public — which is why ST78 could exclude Good Ventures Foundation for FY2025), and a CRT's Form 5227 (public except Schedule A; already moot per ST115).

**NF15.
