 Anthropic’s Seven Billionaire Cofounders Nearly Double Their Fortunes As AI Giant’s Valuation Soars To $380 Billion
 Despite recent digs from rivals Sam Altman and Elon Musk, the Anthropic team is on a hot streak, thanks to a memorable Super Bowl ad campaign, a widely-discussed 20,000-word essay from its CEO and an eye-popping $30 billion fundraise announced Thursday. 
 By Richard Nieva , 
 Forbes Staff.
 Richard Nieva is a Forbes senior writer covering technology. 
 Follow Author 
 Feb 12, 2026, 07:21pm EST Updated Feb 12, 2026, 07:54pm EST 
 Anthropic's CEO Dario Amodei is now worth an estimated $6.8 billion, thanks to a recent funding round that boosted the AI firm's valuation to $380 billion. Amodei has already pledged to give 80% of his fortune away. 
 Copyright 2024 The Associated Press. All rights reserved 
 The artificial intelligence arms race keeps heating up, with AI giant Anthropic announcing Thursday that it raised $30 billion at a $380 billion valuation, up from $183 billion in September. That nearly doubled the fortunes of its seven billionaire cofounders, led by CEO Dario Amodei and his sister Daniela Amodei, to $7 billion apiece, Forbes estimates. 
 Anthropic is still worth less than its rival OpenAI, which private investors valued at $500 billion in October. But Anthropic’s cofounders are richer than OpenAI’s cofounder and CEO Sam Altman, who doesn’t own any direct equity in the AI behemoth and is worth an estimated $3 billion. Forbes estimates that Anthropic’s seven cofounders each own just over 1.8% of the company. 
 In early January, Altman spoke about Anthropic, whose cofounders are all ex-OpenAI employees, with a collegial tone. While OpenAI has targeted consumers with its wildly popular ChatGPT, Anthropic has cornered the market for business users. “I definitely think they do a good job on this one singular focus of ‘we're gonna get the enterprise right,’” Altman told Forbes . “They have a good coding model for sure. I respect it a lot. I think that was a good innovation from them.”
 Just a few weeks later, when Anthropic dropped a series of Super Bowl ads that apparently mocked OpenAI’s choice to include ads in ChatGPT, Altman took the gloves off. “I guess it’s on brand for Anthropic doublespeak to use a deceptive ad to critique theoretical deceptive ads that aren’t real,” he posted on X. “Anthropic serves an expensive product to rich people. We are glad they do that and we are doing that too, but we also feel strongly that we need to bring AI to billions of people who can’t pay for subscriptions.”
 Altman’s suddenly more aggressive posturing might underscore how big of a threat Anthropic has become. Meanwhile, Elon Musk fired his own (baseless) attack on his social media platform X today in response to Anthropic’s funding round announcement, calling the company “evil” and discrimanatory. “Frankly, I don’t think there is anything you can do to escape the inevitable irony of Anthropic ending up being Misanthropic,” he wrote. “You were doomed to this fate when you chose your name.”
 Those rivals can say what they will, but Anthropic’s been on a hot streak of late: The Super Bowl ads were widely praised as biting satire (even Altman conceded he laughed). Its coding prowess has become so vaunted that the early February release of its newest model, Claude Opus 4.6, rattled global software stocks, wiping out billions of dollars of value as investors worried these firms might face an existential threat. 
 And now comes Anthropic’s monster fundraising round, in which the company raised nearly $20 billion more than originally planned. Anthropic declined to comment on the net worths of its founders.
 The Amodei siblings grew up in San Francisco’s Mission District in the 1980s, where Dario became obsessed with math and physics, while Daniela excelled at liberal arts and music. Dario joined Google’s vaunted Google Brain research lab in 2015, before moving to OpenAI a year later and eventually rising to become the ascendant AI lab’s vice president of research. Daniela meanwhile went into politics, working for former Pennsylvania congressman Matt Cartwright. Then after five years at payments giant Stripe, she joined her brother at OpenAI where she served as vice president of safety and policy. 
 In 2021, the Amodei siblings, along with a faction of other defectors, split off from OpenAI after an internal power struggle over AI safety. Alongside cofounders Jack Clark, Sam McCandlish, Chris Olah, Tom Brown and Jared Kaplan, they founded Anthropic, a rival with a particular focus on deploying AI models responsibly. 
 Since then, the company has become a fearsome competitor to OpenAI. Anthropic’s crown jewel has been Claude Code, a version of the Claude chatbot that’s tailor made for computer programming. On Thursday, Anthropic said the product hit an annualized revenue rate of $2.5 billion, up from $1 billion in December . 
 Even better for Anthropic, Claude Code’s traction has been like a gateway drug for its enterprise clients. Over 60% of Anthropic’s business customers are using more than one Claude product, the company told Forbes in November. That includes the Claude Developer Platform for API access, which enables customers to build tools on top of Claude models, and an enterprise grade version of the Claude chatbot, which has greater security features and administrative controls, especially for more highly regulated industries like healthcare or law. When the company noticed this pattern last summer, it began to tweak its products to maximize the trend, experimenting with new pricing structures and bundling packages, Chief Product Officer (and Instagram cofounder) Mike Krieger told Forbes in November.
 The company also announced on Thursday that annualized revenue had climbed to $14 billion, up from $7 billion in October. Meanwhile, Anthropic has also become the most popular vendor in the enterprise language model market, which hit $8.4 billion in 2025, according to a study last summer by Menlo Ventures (which is an Anthropic investor). 
 The spike in wealth for Anthropic’s cofounders is a sign of the company’s strength but it’s particularly notable for another reason. In a more than 20,000 word essay Dario Amodei published last month titled “The Adolescence of Technology,” he sounded the alarm over the threat of money being accumulated by too few. He noted that the fortune of Elon Musk, who is currently worth almost $850 billion according to Forbes, exceeds that of John D. Rockefeller whose fortune represented roughly 2% of US GDP at the height of the Gilded Age. “The thing to worry about is a level of wealth concentration that will break society,” he wrote. To that end, he said all seven Anthropic cofounders have pledged to give away 80% of their wealth to charity. 
 Thanks to Anthropic’s latest funding round, that means the total value of that pledge is now up to $49 billion.
