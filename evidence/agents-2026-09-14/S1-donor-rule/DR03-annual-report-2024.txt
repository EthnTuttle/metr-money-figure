METR
In one page
METR researches, develops and runs evaluations of frontier AI systems’ ability to
complete complex tasks.
This means our work focuses on AI agents. METR’s evaluations indicate potential major impact on the
world by measuring general-purpose tasks like conducting research or developing an app, and
concerning capabilities like an AI agent conducting cyberattacks or making itself hard to shut down.

METR also prototypes governance approaches which use AI systems’ measured or forecasted
capabilities to determine when better risk mitigations are needed for further scaling. This included
prototyping the widely-used Responsible Scaling Policies approach.

Measuring
Ability to automate AI R&D
When AI systems can automate AI R&D, AI capabilities could advance extremely rapidly.  
New dangerous capabilities could emerge much faster than humanity could respond with protective
measures. Tracking when this might occur is a primary focus at METR.




  RE-Bench
                                                             METR developed RE-Bench, a first-of-its-
                                                             kind benchmark measuring performance on
                                                             seven projects representative of work done
                                                             by AI research engineers. It tests the ability
                                                             of AI systems to perform tasks of much
                                                             greater realism than anything before: day-
                                                             long projects like fitting a scaling law or
                                                             designing an AI coding agent.

                                                             Remarkably, we found that frontier AI
                                                             systems can already compete with humans
                                                             on some research engineering tasks. This
                                                             generated exceptional discussion, and was
                                                             taken seriously by notable figures who work
                                                             on AI risks and by skeptics.

                                                             The results were highlighted in the launch of
                                                             the International AI Safety Report.

                                                             We are now working to expand RE-Bench
                                                             and to conduct other experiments. In early
                                                             2025, we published research specifically on
                                                             Measuring Automated Kernel Engineering.
                                                             We found that AI agents can accelerate GPU
                                                             computations about 15x more than
                                                             previously reported.

  Quantifying autonomy


  We shared our first proposal for quantifying autonomy in our example autonomy evaluation
  protocol and used improved methodology to demonstrate initial results in our August update.




  By the end of 2024, simple AI agents could complete about as many self-contained software
  engineering, cybersecurity, ML, research and reasoning tasks as skilled humans who have a time
  limit of very approximately 2 hours per task. They complete 1-hour-long tasks of these types with
  around 50% reliability.

  Tracking whether the agent can complete the task at all is a relevant metric because if an AI agent
  can complete a software engineering, cybersecurity, ML, research or reasoning task at all, it can
  probably complete it for much less cost than a human.



The comparisons to how long tasks take humans are based on people with STEM undergraduate
degrees and technical work experience attempting the same tasks under similar conditions. Gathering
this human comparison data takes considerable work but makes the quantification of autonomous
capabilities far more meaningful.




Strengthening the autonomy task suite
METR doubled its available autonomy tasks to around
200 software engineering, cybersecurity, ML, research
and reasoning tasks. You can see examples here. The
new tasks add diversity and scale to the task suite,
increasing the external and internal validity of
evaluations conducted using it. We also improved the
structure of our tasks, including adding machine-
readable documentation and tests to verify that tasks
work as expected.
As noted above, we gathered data on how long it takes for humans to complete the same
tasks we test AI systems on. As well as enabling meaningful comparison to humans like in the
graphs above, it forms part of our quality assurance process. Many benchmarks include tasks
which are simply impossible or otherwise have major issues, and having a human do the task
catches these cases. We also made process improvements to make contracting humans to
do these tasks highly efficient.

     Looking forward
     In early 2025, we will be publishing research on the rapid growth of autonomy in
     frontier AI systems (something like Moore’s Law for the autonomy of AI systems). 

     This requires augmenting our autonomy task suite, such as adding shorter tasks to
     be able to evaluate older systems (e.g. GPT-2), and deciding how best to interpret
     and extrapolate from the data we have on various AI systems. 

     In parallel, we continue to improve our autonomy task suite, such as investigating
     ways in which it might be insufficiently representative of the real world.

Approach to
Sharing infrastructure and methods
    Open-sourcing our Vivaria evaluation platform
    METR open-sourced Vivaria, the tool on which both our and the AI Security
    Institute’s work on AI agent evaluations were initially built. Vivaria runs AI agents on
    tasks, logs their actions and provides efficient ways to study the results, both at a
    large scale (e.g. databases and dashboards) and at a small scale (e.g. editing a run
    part-way through to see how this would change the results).

Integration of methods into company

and government policy


                                                      In 2023, METR published a proposal for how

                                                      to use capability evaluations to manage risk:

                                                      AI companies should define specific

                                                      capability thresholds by which they must

                                                      have specific safety measures in place before

                                                      developing or deploying such systems. The

                                                      first commitments were made the same year,

                                                      and by early 2025, 9 major AI companies had

                                                      established such policies.



                                                      Anthropic, Google DeepMind, Magic, G42 and

                                                      Amazon credited METR for assistance

                                                      establishing their first policies. Additionally,

                                                      OpenAI, Meta, Microsoft and xAI published

                                                      policies.



                                                      Additionally, to help companies understand

                                                      existing best practices, METR published

                                                      Common Elements of Frontier AI Safety

                                                      Policies and presented at the Conference on

                                                      Frontier AI Safety Frameworks. We expect

                                                      more companies to publish policies, and we

                                                      maintain a list here.



                                                      This proposal is now industry consensus, in

                                                      fact the most consensus AI governance

                                                      approach among major AI companies.
